## What is tested (and what is not)

The screening table now flags a country whose latest estimate is old as *higher last-known burden* when that estimate was in the higher quarter (75th percentile) of countries observed in its own reference year. That is only reasonable if relative standing persists. This evaluation measures, for countries observed again later, how often higher-quarter standing at year T is followed by higher-quarter standing at T + h. The primary horizon is h = 3 (non-overlapping three-year windows).

It does **not** validate 'priority' (no ground truth exists), does not forecast, and cannot observe the future of countries that dropped out of the data.

## Limits

- Persistence of relative standing between observed years is not a forecast of future food insecurity and is not evidence that any screening category improves outcomes.
- Pairs are dependent (same countries across origins and horizons); there are only a few origin years.
- Countries that stopped being observed may differ systematically from those that continued; see the diagnostic table.
- 'Higher quarter' is a relative position among observed estimates, not a policy threshold.
